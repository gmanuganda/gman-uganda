About GMaN

Faith in action. Professional practice. Human dignity at the centre.

GMaN — God's Manifestation among Nations — exists to serve people and communities through compassionate, ethical and evidence-informed work that protects lives and strengthens the systems around them.

Why GMaN exists

We believe meaningful social change requires more than responding to problems after harm occurs. It requires listening, understanding vulnerability, strengthening families and institutions, building capacity and creating practical pathways toward safer, healthier communities.

“Advancing Dignity. Promoting Justice. Protecting Lives.”

GMaN works across social work, child protection, safeguarding, social justice, family strengthening, youth development, disability inclusion, community development, research, monitoring and evaluation, and professional training.

Our approach is collaborative: we complement existing systems, work with relevant stakeholders and support organisations to understand risks, improve practice and develop solutions that can be sustained locally.

Current project

PROJECT BOYCHILD — Protect. Develop. Prepare.

GMaN is currently working on PROJECT BOYCHILD, a child-protection and positive-development programme focused on helping boys and adolescent males grow into responsible, resilient, respectful and socially responsible men.

GMaN project

Protect the Boy. Develop the Boy. Prepare the Man.

PROJECT BOYCHILD recognises that boys have their own protection and developmental needs. The programme creates structured opportunities for emotional development, healthy relationships, leadership, educational motivation, decision-making, child-protection awareness, mentorship, positive masculinity, fatherhood preparation and community engagement.

PROTECTSafety, prevention and help-seeking
DEVELOPEmotional, social and leadership growth
PREPAREResponsible adulthood and family life

The development pathway

IdentifyProtectMentor DevelopEquipPrepare

What the project addresses

Family instability, bullying, harmful peer pressure, school disengagement, violence, negative models of masculinity, difficulty expressing emotions, lack of positive male role models and inadequate preparation for adulthood.

How PROJECT BOYCHILD works

Education Mentorship Life skills Father engagement School engagement Leadership Child protection Community action

Who is involved

Boys, parents, fathers, teachers, school leaders, social workers, mentors, community leaders, youth organisations, child-protection actors and appropriate service providers.

GMaN recognises the particular responsibilities involved in work with children and vulnerable populations. We aim to complement existing systems rather than unnecessarily duplicate them, working with qualified partners and relevant authorities where specialist or statutory intervention is required.
